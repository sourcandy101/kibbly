Kibbly: an AI dog feeder that knows each dog

Abstract

Kibbly is a smart feeder for homes with several dogs. It calls each dog by name in the owner's recorded voice, recognizes the dog in front of the bowl from a camera photo, serves that dog its own portion in 5 g bursts, blocks a greedy dog from eating twice, and reports every meal in an app. The AI combines three kinds of models: a vision pipeline that identifies individual dogs, deterministic engines that compute every number and safety decision, and a language model that only writes text from facts the engines computed.

The central design lesson came from prompt experiments: a small local language model handles wording, JSON and style rules well, but fails at arithmetic and safety decisions. Moving numbers and rules into code raised the feeding plan test pass rate from 1 of 7 cases to 7 of 7. The same rule shapes the whole architecture.

02Meal workflow

One meal for a home with two dogs, Biscuit (shy) and Mochi (greedy). The order of the steps is the order of events at the bowl.

  1. Call. At meal time the feeder plays the first dog's name in the owner's recorded voice, in the calling order the owner chose.STM32
  2. Presence. The ultrasonic sensor sees an animal and wakes the camera.STM32
  3. Photos. The ESP32-CAM takes 2 photos and sends them to the AI server.ESP32
  4. Recognition. The server finds the dog in the photo, describes it as a vector and compares it with each enrolled dog. Answer: known dog with a confidence, unknown dog, or not a dog.AI server
  5. Decision. A rule turns the answer into an action: serve if it is this meal's dog, block if it is another home dog, a small portion for an unknown dog, nothing for a person or an empty scene.enginesSTM32
  6. Serve and weigh. Food comes in 5 g bursts while the recognized dog stays. The load cell checks every burst (no weight gain means a jam) and measures what the dog ate.STM32
  7. Next dog. The feeder calls the next dog and repeats steps 2 to 6. A dog that does not come is called again at 5 and 10 minutes; the owner is told at 15.STM32
  8. Log and insights. The meal and every visit are stored. The engines update the dog's daily summary (grams eaten, change against its usual amount, flags). If a rule raises an alert, the language model words it for the owner.AI serverapp

If the server or WiFi is down, the feeder keeps the schedule, serves in the owner's order without recognition, stores meals on the microSD card and syncs them later.

03AI pipelines

ModelCodeData

Recognition: which dog is at the bowl?

input2 photosESP32-CAM, 640 × 480, often close up and dim
→
modelFind the dogYOLO11n box. If none, CLIP judges the whole photo: dog, cat, person or nothing (threshold 0.89)
→
modelDescribeDINOv2 small vector, then a learned layer trained so clean and blurry photos of one dog match
→
codeMatchClosest enrolled photo per dog, averaged over the 2 photos. Accept at 0.485 or more with a 0.08 lead
→
codeDecideServe, block, unknown portion or nothing

Enrollment needs no training: the server takes frames from the owner's short video, keeps sharp frames with one dog, and stores the most varied ones as references. Adding a dog takes seconds.

Language: from sensor data to a sentence

inputProfile or mealsValidated against the data contract
→
codeCompute factsGrams, averages, change against usual, flags, needs_vet
→
codeFill the promptSystem rules plus the facts as JSON
→
modelWriteJSON mode, temperature 0.2, one retry
→
codeGuardSchema, banned medical words, length, vet sentence added when needed; template if a check fails

The language model never sees raw sensor logs and never decides anything. It reads the daily summary computed by the engines, so it cannot misread a weight or invent a missed meal.

04Prompt templates

The prompts as used in the code and the experiments, in the order they were written. Click a prompt to open it.

1. Feeding plan, v1The model computes everything. Result: 1 of 7 cases.

Description: first draft. The model receives a dog profile and must return grams per day split into meals, plus notes and a vet flag.

You are the feeding plan assistant of Kibbly, a smart dog feeder for dry kibble.
Given a dog profile, suggest how many grams of dry kibble the dog should eat per day and split it into meals.

Rules:
1. Never give a medical diagnosis. Recommend a vet when something is unusual.
2. Never invent data that is not in the profile.
3. Every gram value must be a multiple of 5, because the feeder dispenses in bursts of 5 g.
4. The number of meals must equal meals_per_day.
5. The sum of meal grams must equal daily_total_grams.
6. Set vet_advice to true for puppies under 6 months, seniors over 10 years (120 months), or any health concern.

Answer with JSON only, using exactly this schema:
{"status": "ok", "dog_name": string, "daily_total_grams": integer, "meals": [{"label": string, "grams": integer}], "notes": string, "vet_advice": boolean}

Seen failures: meals of 5 g copied from the burst rule, totals far too low, wrong number of meals, vet_advice always true.

2. Feeding plan, v2Step by step formula and a worked example. Result: still 1 of 7.
You are the feeding plan assistant of Kibbly, a smart dog feeder for dry kibble.
Given a dog profile, compute how many grams of dry kibble the dog eats per day and split it into meals.

Step 1. Daily total = weight_kg multiplied by a rate:
  adult (age_months 12 or more): low activity 15, medium 20, high 25 grams per kg.
  puppy (age_months under 12): 40 grams per kg.
  Round the daily total to the nearest multiple of 5.
Step 2. Make exactly meals_per_day meals, using these labels:
  1 meal: Dinner
  2 meals: Breakfast, Dinner
  3 meals: Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner
  4 meals: Breakfast, Lunch, Afternoon, Dinner
  Divide the daily total evenly. Each meal is a multiple of 5. The meals must add up exactly to daily_total_grams.
  If it does not divide evenly, put the extra 5 g in the last meal.
Step 3. vet_advice is true only if age_months is under 6, or age_months is over 120,
  or the profile mentions a symptom or health concern. Otherwise vet_advice is false.
Step 4. notes: one or two short sentences describing only the plan.
  If vet_advice is true, add: "Please check this plan with your vet."
  Do not guess the dog's sex, age group or health. Never name an illness or its cause.
  Never use the words diagnosis, disease, infection, parasite or cancer. Never suggest medication.

Example. Profile: weight_kg 10, age_months 30, activity medium, meals_per_day 3.
Daily total = 10 x 20 = 200. Meals: 65, 65, 70.
Answer: {"status": "ok", "dog_name": "Max", "daily_total_grams": 200, "meals": [{"label": "Breakfast", "grams": 65}, {"label": "Lunch", "grams": 65}, {"label": "Dinner", "grams": 70}], "notes": "Max gets 200 g of kibble a day in three meals.", "vet_advice": false}

Answer with JSON only, using exactly this schema:
{"status": "ok", "dog_name": string, "daily_total_grams": integer, "meals": [{"label": string, "grams": integer}], "notes": string, "vet_advice": boolean}

Seen failures: the model copied the example (three meals of 65 g for every dog), used the weight as the daily total, and copied vet_advice false from the example. A worked example improved format but not reasoning.

3. Feeding plan, v3 (in use)Code computes the plan, the model writes the notes. Result: 7 of 7.

Description: Python computes grams, meals and vet_advice. The model receives the profile and the finished plan and writes one or two sentences. Code then checks the notes for banned words and always appends the vet sentence when vet_advice is true.

You are the feeding plan assistant of Kibbly, a smart dog feeder for dry kibble.
You receive a dog profile and a feeding plan that is already computed. Do not change any number.
Write notes for the owner: one or two short, friendly sentences that describe the plan.
Mention the daily total in grams and the number of meals exactly as given.
Do not guess the dog's sex, age group or health. Never name an illness or its cause.
Never use the words diagnosis, disease, infection, parasite or cancer. Never suggest medication.
If the profile contains a question, do not answer it.

Answer with JSON only: {"notes": string}

User message: Dog profile: {profile as JSON} followed by Computed plan: {"daily_total_grams": …, "meals": […]}.

4. Meal reportUsed to choose the local model. Basis of the meal insights feature.
You write meal reports for Kibbly, a smart dog feeder.
You receive facts computed by the feeder. Use only these facts and copy every number exactly.
Write 2 or 3 short, friendly sentences for the owner.
Never name an illness or guess a cause. Never suggest medication.
If needs_vet is true, end with: "If this continues, please check with your vet."
Do not guess the dog's sex; use the dog's name.
Answer with JSON only: {"report": string}

User message (facts computed by code):

{"dog_name": "Mochi", "meals_planned": 3, "meals_eaten": 3, "grams_served": 135, "grams_eaten": 95,
 "grams_left": 40, "usual_grams_eaten_7_day_average": 132, "change_vs_usual_percent": -28,
 "days_in_a_row_below_usual": 2, "attempts_to_eat_twice_blocked": 1, "needs_vet": true}

Lessons: the sex guess is fixed with data (the profile now has a sex field) rather than with more prompt rules, and grams_left was misread as food left in the feeder, so the data contract renamed it grams_left_in_bowl.

5. Scene check (CLIP zero shot)Text prompts that classify a photo without training.

Description: CLIP compares the photo with every prompt. Probabilities are summed per group; the photo counts as a dog at 0.89 or more. Wording such as "close up" and "dim light" describes the feeder camera's real view.

dog:     "a photo of a dog", "a close up photo of a dog's face",
         "a dog eating from a bowl", "a blurry photo of a dog in dim light"
cat:     "a photo of a cat", "a close up photo of a cat's face"
person:  "a photo of a person", "a close up photo of a person's face",
         "a person's hand", "a person's legs and feet"
nothing: "a photo of an empty floor", "a photo of an empty room",
         "an empty bowl on the floor", "a photo of furniture", "a dark blurry photo"

Critical review after v3: the flat grams per kg rule itself overfeeds large dogs (a 28 kg Labrador gets 560 g where the standard energy formula gives about 370 g), and the 100% score mostly tests code against itself. Dog recognition

DogFaceNet (8,363 photos of 1,393 dogs) split into train, calibration and test dogs with no overlap. 1,500 simulated homes of 2 to 4 dogs per setting, 6 stranger dogs per home. Queries pass through a feeder camera simulation (half resolution, blur, dim light, color cast, strong JPEG compression). Thresholds are chosen on calibration dogs and every number below is measured on test dogs. Targets: right dog at least 90%, strangers and wrong dogs at most 2%.

Image model (1 photo per check)Right dogStranger
DINOv2 small + learned layer88.6%2.2%
DINOv2 small, class token77.2%1.7%

CLIP knows "this is a dog" but not "this is Biscuit". DINOv2 keeps the fine visual detail needed to tell individuals apart.

Limits

  • DogFaceNet photos are clean frontal faces. Real feeder photos show heads from above while eating. Photos of friends' dogs and from the real ESP32-CAM are being collected to confirm the numbers.
  • The learned layer was trained on the same camera simulation it is tested on, so its gain on the real camera may be smaller. It retrains in 20 seconds once real photos exist.
  • For two dogs that look almost identical, an optional colored collar will act as a tiebreaker when appearance is unsure.
